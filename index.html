Why Lucidity Exists

Where I'm Coming From

I grew up in a French village of 500 people. I had internet access, a decent high school, and ambition. By conventional metrics, I should have had equal opportunities.

The Problem I Noticed

At 15, I'd never heard of liberal arts colleges, the Rhodes Scholarship, research opportunities, or how networking actually works. Not because I lacked intelligence or drive — but because information inequality is invisible. My postal code was determining my future more than my potential.

Students in Paris learn about these opportunities casually, through conversations at dinner tables or with well-connected teachers. Students in rural areas discover them years too late, if at all. This isn't about motivation or talent. It's about knowing what questions to ask — and you can't search for what you don't know exists.

What Lucidity Does

Through rigorous, accessible essays, I'm building the resource I wish I'd had at 15. Lucidity helps young people aged 15-20 develop clarity about education systems, decision-making frameworks, AI literacy, and the skills that actually matter for their futures.

This isn't motivational content. It's analytical, evidence-based writing that makes invisible advantages visible and helps students make informed decisions about their trajectories.

What I'm Still Figuring Out

I'm 16. I don't have all the answers, and I'm not claiming to be an expert. What I do have is recent, lived experience of these information gaps — and the drive to build something that helps others navigate them earlier than I did.

"You can't search for what you don't know exists. Lucidity makes the invisible visible."
